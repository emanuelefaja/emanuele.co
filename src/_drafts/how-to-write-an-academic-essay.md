I've been writing many essays recently for my various academic endeavours, including criminal law and business strategy.

I wanted to leave some notes for my future self on the structure of a good academic essay and the way to write effective paragraphs to expound on my key points.

The structure for any essay will always be as follows:

1.  **Introduction —** This is where you define the essay and then outline what you will be discussing.
2.  **Main Body —** Ideally, this is divided by the critical points that one wants to make, and then reviews key supporting evidence, critically analysis the evidence, and then related everything back to the main topic of the essay (or question).
3.  **Conclusion —** This provides an overall summary and then provides a final verdict.

## On Introductions.

Writing an introduction is a gateway into your thinking and your argument. This is what people are going to read first, and so you'll catch people when they have the most attention — so start strong!

> You don't get a second chance to create a first impression”

So —how do we achieve this?

The first thing to do is that we need to define the concepts that we are going to be discussing.

I'll take the question [that I had to answer](/how-to-use-company-assessment-tools/) for my Interim Assessment in my Organizational Behaviour (OB) Unit for my MBA:

> For this Interim Assessment, you will be required to write an essay, researching and identifying the assessment tools that are available to provide an analysis of the company.  In this course we have discussed the different tools available to provide company analysis.Your reading and research should identify the tools and how they are used.  Please focus on a combination of tools discussed in Unit 2.1 of the OB forum: Expanded 7-S framework; Balanced Scorecard, SWOT & PESTLE analysis.  What are the merits and demerits of each?  

So, the first step is identifying and understanding the keywords in the question. This will then guide us on what we need to do next.

Ok, so let's break this down.

-   **Researching and Identifying / Reading and Research / Identify —** This first set of keywords highlights the importance of research for this paper. Thus we will need to cite several relevant sources when stating facts about the various company assessment tools;.
-   **How they are used —** We do not just describe what the company assessment tools are but also practical steps in using them.
-   **Combination of Tools —** We should not just look at each tool in isolation but consider how they can be combined to understand an organization better.
-   **Merits and Demerits —** We must critically analyze each tool to understand its strengths and weaknesses.

The key thing when defining the concepts in the question, is not to just state what they are, but to define their importance. *Why* is something important? *Why* should anyone care?

The reason why defining the why is important is because it clearly articulates that you know precisely what is going on.

After you have define key concepts, outline what your essay will cover. This sets the examiner/reader up to know what to expect.

I'll showcase the example from my essay.

*Company assessment tools are frameworks that take different inputs to guide structured decision-making. They are essential because better decision-making leads to better organisational outcomes. They provide a clear answer to where, strategically, an organisation is right now. Ideally, they identify the current gaps and next steps required to reach a future vision of success.*

*This is useful in various situations, such as a manager embarking on a new project, a senior executive considering buying another organisation,  and even a new MBA graduate considering joining a specific company. Different tools will be appropriate depending on the situation and data access.*

*This essay will critically analyse four tools. These are the  7-S Mckinsey Framework, Balanced Scorecard, SWOT, and PESTLE. The writer will review the merits and limitations of each tool and how multiple tools can be combined to create a more thorough analysis of an organisation.*

*Additionally, the writer will conclude with a framework for choosing which tools to use based on the type of problem. This is important because managers increasingly suffer from information overload in the modern world. (Hyde et al. 2004).*

Use the first sentence to convey the main message and the second sentence to talk about the critical points that I am going to cover that are going to lead me to that overall final message.

Another point from Cumbria skills:

Shows your reader how you are going to answer the question
 Shows that you understand the issues and their implications
 May include a definition of the topic or key terms (only if needed)
 Indicates the structure of your answer and makes clear the main areas that you are
going to write about
 shows evidence that you have carried out some research by making a reference to
key sources
 only includes totally relevant details
 is concise: 5% of the word count (e.g.100 words of a 2000 word essay)

And also to: "

Although the introduction is the starting point of your essay, it is easiest to write after you
have completed the main body of your assignment. The reason for this is that by then you
will have a better overall feel for which sources are important, what your key themes are,
and the order in which you will address them. This should save you some time."

## The Main Body.

This can be quite difficult, you can overlook the big picture and the general idea. (change this text as it is verbatim from Shaene).

People get list in the details, and you need to outline your essay before.

Focus on a limited number of key points. Obviously, in some cases you have been given a specific number of things to write about, so then discuss the main points *within* that structure as much as possible.

Use bold underlined sentences for this (I forget what they are called, I can look that up in one of the Cumbria sample word essays). Dr Shaene calls these Active Subheadings. Why are these important? It tells what the paragraph is about. Again, this is to convey a lot of information very quickly (same as diagrams).

First sentence is what the paragraph is going to be about. The second sentence is evidence that backs up the point. The third sentence you need to critically analyse that. How good was the study? Is it reliable, valid, any other factors to take into account, could it be improved? Your final sentence should always related to the question.

First and last sentence as the bread of the sandwich, and the middle as the filling.

Examiners love when you provide evidence for your points, and they love it even more when you critically analyse the available evidence.

## On Conclusion.

You need a verdict, it is a proper version of your introduction. You have reviewed the main points, you are now in a position to give a mature, well-rounded response that can actually assume that the reader as read the things.
