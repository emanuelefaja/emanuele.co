[I previously covered the concept of Actus Reus](/actus-reus-part-1/) (*"Guilty Act"*), and today I want to cover the other significant part of criminal liability, Mens Rea.

Mens Rea is Latin for *"Guilty Mind".* The *basic* equation for criminal liability is:

> AR + MR = Criminal Liability

This means that for most crimes, the MR is a critical component of the crime. If the prosecution cannot prove, beyond a reasonable doubt, that the defendant (D) had some type of criminal intent (and we will speak more about the word *intent* later), then the defendant must be found not guilty.

This goes against most people's initial intuitions. Surely, to commit a crime, the mere fact that you have committed a crime is enough to find you guilty? Why do we need to care about your thoughts before and during the criminal action?

In reality, every crime must have a specific MR attached to it. This is because, as Edward Coke pointed out, "*actus reus non facit reum nisi mens sit rea"* — *“an act does not make a person guilty unless (their) mind is also guilty”.*

This is because, as a society, we do not find it fair to hold people criminally liable for actions that do not have intent. If you did not mean for something to happen, and you could not have possibly have foreseen the consequences, is it at all your fault?

Let's discuss the crime of murder as an example. Killing someone is only murder if you intend to kill or cause significant harm (often called *Gross Bodily Harm*).

Let's imagine a scenario where you are walking in the street, and you turn the corner and bump into someone — something that happens probably millions of times per day across the world.

But, this time, it is different. The person you bumped into has some health conditions, and they drop down dead.

Are you guilty of murder?

Intuitively, most people would say no, that is not your fault. And yet, your actions were a substantial reason why that individual died at that specific moment (health conditions notwithstanding).

However, you didn't set out on your walk with the intention of killing that specific individual, and so you would be found not guilty of murder.

## Types of Mens Rea.

And so, this leads us nicely into the discussion of the three types of Mens Rea in the UK common law tradition:

1.  Intention
2.  Recklessness
3.  Negligence
