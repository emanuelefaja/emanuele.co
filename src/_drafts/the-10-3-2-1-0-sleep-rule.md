I saw this online the other day, and I loved how simple yet effective this is:

-   10 hours before bed: No more caffeine.
-   3 hours before bed: No more food or alcohol.
-   2 hours before bed: No more work.
-   1 hour before bed: No more screen time (shut off all phones, TVs and computers).
-   0: The number of times you hit snooze in the morning.

I don't often follow every single one of these rules, but just knowing that they exist and the reasons why they exist can help one make better lifestyle decisions.

Let's briefly break down *why* these rules exist and why they work.

## 10 hours before bed: No more caffeine.

As I am sure you know, coffee has caffeine, which is a stimulant! So, drinking coffee can make it difficult to fall asleep. There is some math here if we account for the half-life of caffeine, which is the time it takes for your body to eliminate half of the consumed caffeine. While this varies from person to person, the half-life is about 5 to 6 hours on average. If you consume 200mg of caffeine at 3 pm, you'll still have about 100mg in your system at 8 or 9 pm and 50mg around 1 or 2 am. So if you have a double shot of espresso at 3 pm, it's as if you drank one shot at 9 pm.

This remaining caffeine can significantly impact your ability to fall asleep and your sleep quality.

But how does it work?

Caffeine keeps you awake by binding to adenosine receptors in the brain, effectively blocking the sleep-promoting effects of adenosine. Adenosine is a neurotransmitter that naturally accumulates in the brain throughout the day, binding to its receptors and causing drowsiness by slowing down neural activity. However, caffeine is structurally similar to adenosine and competes with it for these receptors. When consumed, caffeine binds to the adenosine receptors, preventing adenosine from binding and exerting its sleep-inducing effects. This increases neural activity, resulting in heightened alertness, improved concentration, and reduced fatigue.

Moreover, by blocking the adenosine receptors, caffeine prevents the natural build-up of sleep pressure that typically accumulates during the day. This is why you feel more alert and awake after consuming caffeine. Caffeine also stimulates the release of other neurotransmitters, such as dopamine and norepinephrine, which further enhance alertness and cognitive performance. The combination of these effects results in increased wakefulness and improved mental function that many people experience after consuming caffeine.
