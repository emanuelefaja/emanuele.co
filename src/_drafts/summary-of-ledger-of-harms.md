I watched The Social Dilemma when it came out, and it led me to rethink my relationship with technology, especially with social media.

I then deleted both my Instagram and Facebook accounts on my 30th birthday, and I haven't looked back since. 18 months later, I can say that it has been a very positive change in my life, and I have taken other steps to reduce the impact of technology, such as removing all notifications from my phone and using a [Chrome extension to remove all the extra fluff from YouTube](https://unhook.app/) which includes the recommendation engine.

The key benefit of these changes is that I have been able to focus more on my life, instead of constantly being an observer of other people's lives.

I am considering taking things a step further, and also deleting my LinkedIn account as well. While it is mildly useful for work, one always has to weigh up the costs associated with any amount of technology. I don't use it very much, but occasionally when I do use it I find myself to be irresistibly drawn to the infinite scroll feed, and I fall down the rabbit hole.

I never feel better after one of these sessions, and thus I am considering leaving. It is easy to think that someone as "trivial" as social media cannot be that harmful, but evidence shows the opposite. It can be extremely detrimental to living a good life.

The people behind The Social Dilemma set up the [Center for Humane Technology](https://www.humanetech.com/), and I was introduced to their [Ledger of Harms](https://ledger.humanetech.com/) while working on the set of Digital Standards for the United Nations Development Fund.

I wanted to create a simple summary for myself so I can easily remember these in the future.

Let's take the reasoning behind creating the Ledger of Harms directly from their webpage:

> Under immense pressure to prioritize engagement and growth, technology platforms have created a race for human attention that’s unleashed invisible harms to society. Here are some of the costs that aren't showing up on their balance sheets.

And the summary:

1.  **The Next Generations —** From developmental delays to suicide, children face a host of physical, mental, and social challenges.
2.  **Making Sense of the World —** Misinformation, conspiracy theories, and fake news.
3.  **Attention and Cognition —** Loss of crucial abilities including memory and focus.
4.  **Physical and Mental Health —** Stress, loneliness, feelings of addiction, and increased risky health behavior.
5.  **Social Relationships —** Less empathy, more confusion, and misinterpretation.
6.  **Politics and Elections —** Propaganda, distorted dialogue, and a disrupted democratic process.
7.  **Systemic Oppressions —** Amplification of racism, sexism, homophobia, and ableism.
8.  **Do Unto Others —** Many people who work for tech companies — and even the CEOs — limit tech usage in their own homes.

## **The Next Generations**.

Children who have significant exposure to digital technology suffer from significant long-term consequences in their development. It is easy to forget that *anything* that you do actually changes your brain's structure.

The incredible thing about our brains is that they have neuroplasticity, a term that refers to the brain's ability to modify, change, and adapt both structure and function throughout life and in response to experience.

This is even more important for children, who have higher levels of neuroplasticity than adults. This is why you can learn a language effortlessly when you are a young child, simply by absorbing what everyone is saying around you, but as an adult, you need to make a significant effort to do so.

Harms to children include bullying, sexual interactions with adults online, and increased risk of suicide. The last point is showcased in the following graph by the Center for Disease Control and Prevention in the US.

It is interesting that the lines start to move upwards significantly since 2008 — which correlates to when smartphones and social media first went mainstream.
