In UNDP, Delivery means that amount of money (often called "resources") that are spent on programmes that attempt to help people all over the world This is in contrast to money that is spent on the day-to-day activities of running UNDP, such as paying salaries, electricity, and office leases.

Today, we are going to be reviewing the delivery data for all countries that UNDP operates in, to see if we unearth some interesting insights.

First things first, let's get total delivery year on year from 2018 to 2022.

![](/media/2023/08/total-undp-delivery-1024x648.png)

Overall, this looks good. Although 2019 and 2022 there was a significant drop, 2022 was a record year for delivery.

However, we have to account inflations, and we can get the global inflation figures from Statista.\[mfn\][Statista: Global Inflation Rate](https://www.statista.com/statistics/256598/global-inflation-rate-compared-to-previous-year/)\[/mfn\]

Inflation happens when there is more money chasing fewer goods. There has been pressure on both sides of this equation in recent years, with the US printing a lot of dollars, thus increasing the money supply, and also with global supply chain issues due to COVID19 causing a shortage of goods.

So let's look at this data again, accounting for the compounding inflation and thus discounting the dollar amount.

We do this quite easily:

![](/media/2023/08/total-delivery-undp-with-inflation-1024x648.png)

This shows a very different picture. The non-inflation-adjusted total delivery for 2022 was approximately $4,809,510,190. This means that in nominal terms (i.e., not taking into account the effects of inflation), the total delivery increased from 2018 to 2022.

However, when adjusted for inflation, the total delivery in 2022 had less purchasing power than the total delivery in 2018. The inflation-adjusted total delivery decreased by approximately 13.16% from 2018 to 2022.

To have the same purchasing power in 2022 as in 2018, taking into account the effects of inflation, the total delivery would have needed to be approximately $5,738,774,287.

The gap between the required total delivery to maintain the same purchasing power as in 2018 and the actual total delivery in 2022 is approximately $929,264,097.
