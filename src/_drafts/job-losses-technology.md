I have been wondering lately about the types of jobs that will be lost as technology improves in this century.

We like to pretend that this is a new problem, but of course there is something that's happened throughout human history. Just ask anyone who used to work as a phone operator for instance.

However, with the rise of artificial intelligence, there is a question of this time it will be different or not. The key difference being will the job losses come all at once and destabilise society, or will they be gradually enough where people have time to retrain and we will also create brand-new jobs that didn't exist even 10 or 20 years ago.

Personally, I am quite confident that we will find a good solution to this. This is because even if artificial intelligence can do a lot there are still considerations that it has to fit within the broader system of society, and a lot of companies and notoriously slow to uptake new technologies because it's not just about the technology itself, but it's about, the processes, the culture, and the fact that a lot of managers will only pull the trigger once they see everybody else is doing it

I am reminded of the quote about nobody ever getting fired for choosing IBM!

However, we do have to see this from the multiple perspectives. Let's imagine that in five years from now all truck driving will be automated and it's safer and more carbon efficient et cetera. It may take longer than this of course, but there is no doubt that and say 100 years this is for sure going to be the case. So what should truck drivers do? Well, it really depends on how quickly self driving trucks become feasible and can be adopted.

If it's very fast, then perhaps poly makers can step into blunt the effect that there's new technology does have on this particular workforce. It is reasonable to expect that companies would switch to self driving trucks because they are fast and overall cheaper to run and so they will have more profits.

And so the government could mandate that if truck drivers are fired and replaced with self driving trucks then the company should give a few years of wages to the truck driver, which the government itself can loan to the company at the rally low interest rate. This loan can easily be repaid with the additional profits and cost savings that the self driving trucks generate.

Ironically, if the speed of change is not very radical, this may actually be worse for truck drivers because they will find themselves replaced bit by bit and will unlikely have the support of policy makers because it's only a few job losses here and there instead of hundreds of thousands of people out of work all at the same time.

Another thing worth highlighting is looking at how many people used to work in agriculture before compared to now. I believe that in modern economies roughly one percent of the population works to grow the food for everybody else. This is a critical function, but it can be done by so a few people because we managed to introduce technologies, to drastically improve productivity in that sector over the years this means we don't need 9 out of 10 people working in agriculture to grow enough food for a country to survive.

And this means a lot of people can do other jobs that technically speaking are not 100% required for the survival of a country but are extremely useful for a modern society!
